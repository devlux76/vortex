## C. Conferences

Shared conferences are now reachable behind `--semantic`: `join`, `post`, `leave`, `conferences`, and durable formation, arrival, departure, message and closure events (`0b80365`). The older invite/accept pair remains for direct exchanges. Conference membership is in memory; saved events survive restart.

* [x] **C1. Conference descriptor** (§6.1). Signed descriptors carry the same room handle, namespace/profile, embedded topic vector, host, count, capacity, admission, creation/expiry and ordered positive safe-integer revision. Host-only topic revision preserves membership and lifetime, retains source text locally (including lazy formation after interest withdrawal), stages signing atomically and sends authenticated descriptor updates to members. Receivers reject forged, foreign-room, stale and immutable-field changes. Room/generation guards cover signing and fanout; missing revision source is rejected. CLI/API revise and MCP vortex_revise are available; public room status includes revision. `3c307b0`; 410 Bun tests, typecheck and whitespace checks passed.
* [x] **C2. Lazy formation from an open interest** (§6.2). `f1bb5c9`, service/event wiring `0b80365`, with a three-service integration test. * [x] **C3. Explicit creation, joining, and admission** (§6.2, §6.3, §10.3). `create`/`vortex_create` establishes a signed hosted room from a disclosure-checked embedded statement, with capacity 2–256 and open, invitation or authenticated-group admission. * [x] **C4. Star topology with more than two participants** (§7.3). `f1bb5c9`, service integration and three-peer fan-out proof `0b80365`. Implemented: each participant holds one stream to the host, which fans messages out; duplicates are discarded by message id after a reconnection.

## F. Screening and abuse

* [ ] **F1. Content screen** (§10.8, §11.1). Nothing exists. It is a later piece of work in Elixir, with the immune modules of a separate local agent project as the pattern to port; it is not part of the wake router and does not go into it. Done: route, scent, and recurrence signals; results `clear`, `odd`, `held`; held notices carrying no peer text; `vortex_held` with read, summarize, discard; reference sets seeded by the operator and grown by adjudication; a local journal; the ring rules for held messages; an outbound check; unscreened delivery marked as such when the screen fails. Split into several items when work starts.
* [ ] **F2. Summaries of held messages** (§10.8). A reader model with no tools, credentials, or memory that describes a message in the third person. Either a small local model in the service or a restricted sub-agent supplied by the integration.
* [ ] **F3. Hostile-matching mitigations** (§10.6). Beyond the recomputation in B9: down-weight peers that answer an implausible share of announcements, and an operator allow-list of host identities for joins.
* [ ] **F4. Resource limits** (§10.7). Present: size limits on announcements, direct messages, and API requests, and a cap of 100 known peers. Missing: announcement frequency, subscription count and lifetime, conference membership, retained history, results per announcement, and stream lifetime.
* [ ] **F5. End-to-end group encryption** (§10.4). The host can read everything. Done: MLS (RFC 9420) at the conference layer as an option. Low priority while every host is one of the operator's own services.
* [ ] **F6. Reciprocal accounting with bilateral IOUs** (§10.9). Nothing exists; any peer may ask any other for model work without limit. Done: a peer that has done work states its token estimate and receives an IOU signed by the requester; each service keeps a private ledger per peer of what it owes and is owed; a request can present an IOU, which is honored once and in part; a service declines work by a policy over that ledger; and the operator's listed agents are exempt by identity, never by address. An IOU presented by anyone but its holder, or twice, is refused. Not needed while every peer is one of the operator's own agents; needed before a remote peer, and before the browser extension joins a remote mesh (D6). The amounts are each owner's policy, not protocol constants: the allowance for a stranger, the most one peer may owe, how long a signed IOU stays good, and the tolerance for a stated cost. Done also means an owner can see and set them and see each peer's balance: in the service's configuration and CLI, and in the browser extension's settings (D6). What that interface looks like is the open design question.
